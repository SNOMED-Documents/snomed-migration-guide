# Riverside Community Hospital - Starter migration, allergy data

**Organisation.** A 300-bed community hospital with 15 years of legacy clinical data. No SNOMED CT expertise on staff at the start of the project. A constrained budget and a regional interoperability deadline three years away.

**Approach.** The clinical informatics lead proposed beginning with a single, safety-critical data type: allergies. The rationale was twofold - drug-allergy alerts had been failing in the legacy system, and a single data type could prove the migration approach without overcommitting the organisation. The project plan fit in a single paragraph.

**Terminology strategy.** Reference terminology for the first phase. The legacy allergy records were mapped to SNOMED CT allergen concepts via a pre-built mapping table. Clinicians continued using the existing allergy capture interface.

**System and data migration.** Behind-the-scenes migration. No clinical workflow changes on go-live day.

**Outcomes at 18 months.** Allergy data converted to SNOMED CT. Drug-allergy alerts firing correctly for the first time in years. Allergy data shared with regional partners in a standards-compliant format. A plan for Year 2 - extending to medications and active diagnoses - approved by the executive sponsor.

**Lessons.** A contained first phase with a concrete clinical benefit (correcting a known alert failure) built organisational confidence and secured funding for subsequent phases. Choosing a data type where the business case was patient-safety-driven, not just compliance-driven, made approval straightforward.
