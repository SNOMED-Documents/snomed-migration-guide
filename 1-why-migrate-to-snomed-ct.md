# Why Migrate to SNOMED CT

SNOMED CT migration is not an end in itself. The outcomes that drive organisations to migrate, such as safer clinical decision support, standards-based interoperability, AI-ready data infrastructure, and more - all depend on the data underneath having the right structure and clinical meaning. Eight strategic objectives describe what a well-executed migration makes possible.

## The eight strategic objectives

1. **Comprehensive integration.** SNOMED CT works throughout the clinical information ecosystem: at data entry, in storage, at exchange, and in retrieval. A migration that embeds SNOMED CT consistently across these layers - rather than only at the point of exchange - delivers the most durable benefits.
2. **AI and advanced clinical capabilities.** AI clinical assistants, ambient documentation tools, predictive analytics, and population health monitoring all depend on semantic depth in the underlying data. SNOMED CT's concept model and relationships provide this in ways that flat code lists cannot. This objective is growing in strategic importance faster than any other.
3. **Standards-based interoperability.** Sending and receiving clinical data with partner organisations, national programmes, health information exchanges, and payer systems. FHIR combined with SNOMED CT has become the dominant interoperability standard in most national programmes. Migrated data becomes part of the wider network rather than remaining isolated inside a single system.
4. **Legacy data preservation.** Historical clinical data retains its value after migration when it is converted accurately, context is preserved, original records are retained alongside SNOMED CT-coded versions, and provenance is documented for every conversion. Well-executed migration is auditable and correctable. Poorly executed migration creates data of lower quality than what it replaced.
5. **Data quality and governance.** SNOMED CT's 350,000+ concepts require governance to be used consistently. Mature implementations establish coding quality dashboards, automated validation, consistency metrics, and terminology governance processes - not at go-live, but throughout the operational life of the system.
6. **Version management.** SNOMED CT releases internationally twice per year (January and July). Most national release centres publish national extensions on the same cycle. Mature implementations have processes for impact assessment, staged deployment, and rollback when each new release arrives.
7. **Specialty support.** SNOMED CT implementation guides, standardised value sets, and specialty-specific templates support diverse clinical workflows while preserving the interoperability that standardisation enables.
8. **User adoption and change management.** Clinical and operational staff need training, ongoing support, and feedback mechanisms. Change management is consistently undervalued in migration programmes and consistently identified as a root cause when they fail.

## A realistic starting point

Successful migrations do not typically begin with all eight objectives in scope simultaneously. A contained, clinically-meaningful first phase - migrating a single high-value data type such as allergies, where the clinical benefit is concrete and the scope is manageable - builds the evidence base, the team competence, and the organisational confidence needed for subsequent phases.

The case studies in the Case Studies section illustrate a range of approaches, from minimum-viable regulatory-compliance migrations to multi-year programmes covering entire health authorities.
