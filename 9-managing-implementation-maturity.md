# Managing SNOMED CT Implementation Maturity

SNOMED CT implementation is not a one-time project. SNOMED CT releases twice yearly. Clinical workflows change. New systems are added that need SNOMED CT access. Organisational ambition typically grows as clinical confidence builds. Mature implementations have ongoing processes to support this continuous development.

**Ongoing processes in mature implementations include:**

Release management: impact assessment before each biannual release, testing in a staging environment, staged deployment, rollback capability, and notification to clinical teams of any changes affecting their workflows.

Value-set and subset governance: regular review of locally-authored value sets and subsets against current SNOMED CT content, with a process for updating them when relevant concepts are added, inactivated, or re-organised.

Data quality monitoring: automated detection of coding quality issues in live data, with feedback loops to clinical teams and, where appropriate, to the EHR vendor.

Terminology governance: a responsible team or individual for SNOMED CT decisions within the organisation, with clear authority and a documented process for handling requests, exceptions, and escalations.

## The Implementation Maturity Framework as an ongoing tool

SNOMED International's Implementation Maturity Framework provides a structured self-assessment across the key performance areas of SNOMED CT implementation, tailored separately for NRCs, user organisations, and vendors. The framework is designed for repeated use.

Completing the assessment at the start of a migration establishes a baseline. Returning to it at significant milestones - after data migration is complete, after enabling FHIR exchange, after extending to a new clinical domain - gives a structured view of progress and identifies where the next investment will have the highest impact.

The framework is most valuable as a continuous planning and improvement tool rather than a one-time starting-point assessment. An organisation in the early stages of a migration can use it to focus on the performance areas most relevant to that stage. An organisation three years into a mature implementation can use it to identify where the next phase of development should concentrate.
